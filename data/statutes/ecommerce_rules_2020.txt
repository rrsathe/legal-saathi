### Rule 1 - Short Title and Commencement

(1) These rules may be called the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020.
(2) They shall come into force on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette (23rd July, 2020).

### Rule 2 - Scope and Applicability

(1) Save as otherwise provided in sub-rule (2), these rules shall apply to:—
(a) all goods and services bought or sold over digital or electronic network including digital products;
(b) all models of e-commerce, including marketplace and inventory approaches of e-commerce;
(c) all e-commerce retail, including multi-channel single brand retailers and single brand retailers in single or multiple formats; and
(d) all forms of unfair trade practices across all models of e-commerce:

Provided that these rules shall not apply to any activity of a natural person carried out in a personal capacity not being part of any regular professional or commercial activity.

(2) These rules shall also apply to an e-commerce entity which is not established in India, but systematically offers goods or services to consumers in India.

### Rule 3 - Definitions

(1) In these rules, unless the context otherwise requires,—
(b) "e-commerce entity" means any person who owns, operates or manages digital or electronic facility or platform for electronic commerce, but does not include a seller on a marketplace e-commerce entity;
(c) "digital products" means goods which are licensed or sold with right to access or download or use in digital form;
(e) "inventory e-commerce entity" means an e-commerce entity which owns the inventory of goods or services and sells such goods or services directly to the consumers and shall include single brand retailers and multi-channel retailers;
(f) "grievance officer" means an officer appointed by the e-commerce entity for the consumer grievance redressal under sub-rule (4) of rule 4;
(g) "marketplace e-commerce entity" means an e-commerce entity which provides an information technology platform on a digital or electronic network to facilitate transactions between buyers and sellers;
(j) "user" means any person who accesses or avails any computer resource of an e-commerce entity.

### Rule 4 - Duties of E-Commerce Entities

(1) An e-commerce entity shall be a company incorporated under the Companies Act, 1956 or Companies Act, 2013, or a registered partnership or limited liability partnership, or have a principal office and registered agent in India.
(2) An e-commerce entity shall not adopt any unfair trade practice, whether in the course of business on its platform or otherwise.
(3) Display in a clear and accessible manner on its platform:
(a) legal name of the e-commerce entity;
(b) principal geographic address of its headquarters and all branches;
(c) name and details of its website;
(d) direct contact details including email address, fax, landline and mobile numbers of customer care and Grievance Officer.

(4) Mandatory Grievance Redressal Mechanism:
Every e-commerce entity shall—
(a) appoint a Grievance Officer for consumer grievance redressal and display his name, designation and contact details prominently on its platform;
(b) ensure that the Grievance Officer acknowledges the receipt of any consumer complaint within forty-eight (48) hours;
(c) ensure that the Grievance Officer redresses the complaint within one month (30 days) from the date of receipt of the complaint;
(d) provide a ticket number for each complaint lodged through which the consumer can track the status of the complaint.

(5) Nodal Officer:
Every e-commerce entity shall appoint a nodal person of contact or an alternate senior designated functionary who is resident in India, available 24x7 for coordination with law enforcement agencies and officers.

(8) Cancellation Charges Parity:
No e-commerce entity shall impose cancellation fees on consumers after confirming purchase, unless similar charges are also borne by the e-commerce entity if they cancel the purchase order unilaterally for any reason.

(9) Explicit Consent & Prohibition of Pre-Ticked Checkboxes:
Every e-commerce entity shall record the explicit consent of consumers for the purchase of any good or service. Consent shall not be recorded automatically, including in the form of pre-ticked checkboxes.

(10) Timely Refunds:
Every e-commerce entity shall effect all refund requests within a reasonable period of time, or as per the guidelines prescribed by the Reserve Bank of India or other competent authority.

(11) Absolute Prohibition of Price Manipulation:
No e-commerce entity shall manipulate the price of the goods or services offered on its platform in such a manner as to gain unreasonable profit by imposing unjustified prices, or discriminate between consumers of the same class or make any arbitrary classification of consumers affecting their rights.

### Rule 5 - Liabilities of Marketplace E-Commerce Entities

(1) A marketplace e-commerce entity which seeks exemption from liability under sub-section (1) of section 79 of the Information Technology Act, 2000 shall comply with sub-sections (2) and (3) of that section, and shall strictly comply with all provisions of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 and these rules.
(2) Every marketplace e-commerce entity shall display prominently to its users:
(a) details regarding the sellers offering goods and services, including the name of their business, whether registered or not, their geographic address, customer care number, and ratings/feedback;
(b) a ticket number for tracking complaint status.
(3) Every marketplace e-commerce entity shall provide in a clear and accessible manner on its platform:
(a) information relating to return, refund, exchange, warranty and guarantee, delivery and shipment, modes of payment, and grievance redressal mechanism;
(b) all information required to be provided under the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011.
(4) Mandatory Country of Origin Disclosure:
Every marketplace e-commerce entity shall display the country of origin of the goods offered for sale to enable the consumer to make an informed purchase decision.
(5) Mandatory Expiry Date Disclosure:
Every marketplace e-commerce entity shall ensure that the expiry date or best before date of goods having defined shelf life is clearly exhibited to consumers prior to purchase.

### Rule 6 - Duties of Sellers on Marketplace

(1) No seller shall adopt any unfair trade practice whether in the course of business on the marketplace platform or otherwise.
(2) Every seller offering goods or services through a marketplace e-commerce entity shall:
(a) have a prior written contract with the marketplace entity;
(b) disclose total price in single figure of any good or service, along with breakup price showing all mandatory charges, taxes, and shipping fees.

(3) STRICT STATUTORY PROHIBITION AGAINST REFUSAL OF RETURN OR REFUND:
No seller on marketplace shall refuse to take back goods, or withdraw or discontinue services purchased or agreed to be purchased, or refuse to refund consideration paid, if:
(a) such goods or services are defective, deficient or spurious; or
(b) the characteristics or features of the goods or services do not conform to the representations made by the seller; or
(c) delivery of goods or services is delayed beyond the agreed timeline (other than for force majeure events).

(4) Prohibition on Deceptive Practices and Fake Reviews:
No seller on marketplace shall:
(a) falsely represent itself as a consumer and post reviews about goods or services;
(b) misrepresent the quality or the features of any goods or services.
(5) Sellers must provide an address and contact details to ensure customer service and grievance redressal.

### Rule 7 - Duties of Inventory E-Commerce Entities

(1) Every inventory e-commerce entity shall provide in a clear and accessible manner on its platform:
(a) total price in single figure along with breakup of all mandatory charges, taxes, delivery fees;
(b) information relating to return, refund, exchange, warranty and guarantee, delivery and shipment, and grievance redressal mechanism.
(2) Ensure that notices of return, exchange, or refund are processed smoothly without creating arbitrary hurdles for consumers.

(4) STRICT PROHIBITION AGAINST REFUSAL OF RETURN OR REFUND (INVENTORY ENTITIES):
No inventory e-commerce entity shall refuse to take back goods, or withdraw or discontinue services purchased or agreed to be purchased, or refuse to refund consideration paid, if:
(a) such goods or services are defective, deficient or spurious; or
(b) the characteristics or features of the goods or services do not conform to the representations made by the entity; or
(c) delivery of goods or services is delayed beyond the agreed timeline.

(5) An inventory e-commerce entity shall not engage in false or misleading advertising or make false claims about the quality, standard, or efficacy of its products.

### Rule 8 - Contravention of Rules

The provisions of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 shall apply for any violation of the provisions of these rules.

Any contravention of these rules shall be deemed to be an unfair trade practice under Section 2(47) of the Act and shall attract:
(1) Corrective orders, product recalls, and price reimbursements under Section 20 of the Act by the Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA).
(2) Penalties for false or misleading advertising under Section 21 of the Act.
(3) Penalties for manufacturing, storing, distributing, or selling adulterated or spurious goods under Sections 90 and 91 of the Act.
(4) Compensation, replacement, and damages ordered by the District, State, or National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission under Section 39 of the Act.
